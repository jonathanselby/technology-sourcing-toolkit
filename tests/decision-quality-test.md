# Decision Quality Test

Use this before approving a sourcing recommendation.

A credible decision should answer **yes** to most of these and explicitly explain any exception.

## Problem

- Is the decision framed as an outcome rather than a product search?
- Are hard constraints visible?
- Is the time horizon clear?

## Options

- Were build, buy, rent and retention considered where plausible?
- Were non-viable options removed for explicit reasons?

## Evidence

- Are material supplier claims linked to evidence?
- Are proven, inferred and unknown points distinguishable?
- Could an unresolved claim reverse the decision?

## Architecture and operations

- Is there a defensible technical baseline?
- Are integrations, data, resilience and security boundaries understood?
- Is the continuing operating burden explicit?
- Are required skills and ownership clear?

## Commercial

- Does comparison include implementation, operation and exit rather than licence price only?
- Are usage assumptions visible?
- Has more than one usage/growth scenario been tested?

## Dependency and exit

- Are important dependencies identified?
- Is there a credible exit path?
- Has any part of that path been demonstrated or evidenced?

## Decision

- Are decisive evidence and material assumptions explicit?
- Are accepted risks named?
- Is there a review trigger?
- Could a reviewer in two years understand why the decision was reasonable?

## Failure signals

Stop and investigate when:

- numerical scoring hides weak evidence;
- the preferred option was chosen before requirements were framed;
- commercial and architecture teams are comparing different assumptions;
- exit is described only as a contractual clause;
- a supplier adjective is being treated as a technical fact;
- internal operating cost is assumed to be zero;
- switching cost is omitted because it occurs later.
